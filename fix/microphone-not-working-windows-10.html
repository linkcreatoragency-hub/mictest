Microphone Not Working on Windows 10

The Windows 10 settings layout differs from Windows 11 in a few important places. These are the steps in the order that resolves it fastest, with the exact paths for Windows 10.

Windows 10 splits microphone control across two different settings systems: the modern Settings app and the older Sound control panel inherited from earlier versions. Some options exist in only one of them, which is why following a Windows 11 guide on a Windows 10 machine often leads nowhere.

Run the microphone test above first so you have a baseline, then work down the list. Most cases are resolved by step three.

Fixes, in the order to try them

  1. Check for a hardware mute

    Headset cables, USB microphone bodies and many laptop keyboards carry a mute control that Windows knows nothing about. Look for a microphone icon on your function keys and for an inline switch on any headset cable.

  2. Enable microphone access in Privacy

    Open Settings, then Privacy, then Microphone. Under “Allow access to the microphone on this device”, click Change and set it to On. Then turn on “Allow apps to access your microphone”. Scroll to the bottom for “Allow desktop apps to access your microphone” — this separate switch controls browsers and most installed programs, and it is the one that usually blocks everything.

  3. Set the correct default device

    Right-click the speaker icon in the taskbar and choose “Open Sound settings”. Under Input, choose your microphone from the dropdown, then speak and watch the “Test your microphone” bar move. If it does not move, try each device in the list.

  4. Enable the device in the Sound control panel

    In Sound settings, click “Sound Control Panel” on the right, then open the Recording tab. Right-click in the empty space and tick both “Show Disabled Devices” and “Show Disconnected Devices”. If your microphone appears greyed out, right-click it and choose Enable, then Set as Default Device.

  5. Raise levels and add boost

    Still in the Recording tab, double-click your microphone and open the Levels tab. Set Microphone to 100. If a Microphone Boost slider is present, raise it to +10 or +20 dB. Small increments matter here — too much boost amplifies hiss along with your voice.

  6. Turn off exclusive mode and enhancements

    In the same properties window, open the Advanced tab and untick “Allow applications to take exclusive control of this device”. Then open the Enhancements tab, if present, and tick “Disable all sound effects”. Exclusive mode is a frequent cause of a microphone that works in one app and is dead in every other.

  7. Restart the audio services

    Press Windows+R, type services.msc, press Enter, then restart both “Windows Audio” and “Windows Audio Endpoint Builder”. Set both to Automatic startup if they are not already.

  8. Update or roll back the driver

    Open Device Manager, expand “Audio inputs and outputs”, right-click the microphone and choose Update driver. If the fault started right after a driver update, choose Properties instead and use “Roll Back Driver” on the Driver tab — that button is only available if a previous version is still stored.

Exclusive mode: the fix people never find

Windows lets an application request exclusive control of an audio device, which locks every other program out. It exists so that professional audio software can get the lowest possible latency, and it causes real trouble on ordinary machines.

The symptom is very specific: the microphone works perfectly in one application, and every other application reports it as missing or silent — often with no error message at all. Unticking the exclusive control option in the Advanced tab resolves it permanently, and there is no downside for normal use.

Two settings apps, one microphone

Windows 10 shows microphone settings in both the Settings app and the legacy Sound control panel, and they do not expose the same options. Levels, boost, exclusive mode, enhancements and the ability to enable a disabled device all live only in the old control panel.

The quickest route there is to right-click the speaker icon in the taskbar, choose Open Sound settings, and click “Sound Control Panel” in the right-hand column. Bookmark that path — most stubborn microphone problems are solved on the Recording tab it opens.

Realtek and vendor audio software

Many Windows 10 laptops ship with a vendor audio suite layered on top of the Microsoft driver: Realtek Audio Console, Nahimic, Waves MaxxAudio, Dell Audio or similar. These have their own microphone settings that override the Windows ones, including noise suppression that can gate speech entirely.

If a microphone behaves inconsistently, open that vendor application and either disable its microphone effects or reset it to defaults. If the software has been uninstalled but its driver remains, the effects can persist with no interface to turn them off, in which case reinstalling the vendor package to disable them properly is the cleanest path.

Microphone Not Working on Windows 10 — FAQ

Where is the microphone privacy setting in Windows 10?

Settings, then Privacy, then Microphone in the left column. Remember the third switch near the bottom for desktop apps — it controls browsers and most installed software.

How do I enable a greyed-out microphone?

Open the Sound control panel, go to the Recording tab, right-click in the empty area, tick “Show Disabled Devices”, then right-click your microphone and choose Enable.

Why is my microphone very quiet on Windows 10?

Set the Levels slider to 100 and add Microphone Boost of +10 to +20 dB in the device properties. If it is still quiet, the microphone may be plugged into a line-in jack rather than a microphone jack.

What does exclusive mode do?

It lets one application lock a device for itself. Turning it off in the Advanced tab restores the microphone for every other program and has no downside for normal use.

Should I roll back or update the driver?

If the microphone stopped working right after an update, roll back. If it has never worked or has been failing for a long time, update — or uninstall the device and restart to let Windows reinstall it cleanly.
