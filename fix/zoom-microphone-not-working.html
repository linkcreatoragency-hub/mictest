Zoom Microphone Not Working

Before changing anything in Zoom, confirm the microphone itself works using the test above. Then work through these fixes, which are ordered by how often each one turns out to be the answer.

The most common Zoom microphone problem is not a broken microphone at all — it is a participant who never joined the meeting's audio, or who is muted and cannot see it. Both are fixed in one click once you know where to look.

After those, the usual causes are Zoom holding an input device that is no longer connected and Zoom's automatic volume adjustment fighting a quiet microphone. Everything below assumes the test at the top of this page shows a moving meter.

Fixes, in the order to try them

  1. Join audio and check the mute button

    At the bottom-left of the meeting window, look at the microphone icon. If it says “Join Audio”, you are in the meeting visually but not connected to its audio at all — click it and choose “Join with Computer Audio”. If it shows a red slash, you are muted; click to unmute. Holding the space bar unmutes temporarily, which is a useful way to check quickly.

  2. Select the right microphone in Zoom

    Click the small arrow beside the microphone icon. A list of inputs appears with a tick beside the current one. Choose the device you are actually speaking into. Zoom holds on to a previous selection even after that device is unplugged, which is why calls stop working after you switch headsets.

  3. Run Zoom's own audio test

    Open Settings, then Audio, and press “Test Mic”. Speak, then let it play back. If you hear yourself, Zoom is receiving audio correctly and the problem is elsewhere — most likely mute state or the meeting host's settings.

  4. Turn off automatic volume adjustment

    In Settings, Audio, untick “Automatically adjust microphone volume”, then raise the Input Level slider manually while watching the meter as you speak. Zoom's automatic gain can drive a quiet microphone down to nothing and leave it there.

  5. Set background noise suppression to Low

    In Settings, Audio, open Advanced (or find “Suppress background noise”) and choose Low instead of Auto or High. Aggressive suppression can classify a soft voice as noise and remove it entirely, which listeners hear as you cutting in and out.

  6. Grant Zoom microphone permission

    On Windows, open Settings, Privacy & security, Microphone, and ensure both microphone access and desktop app access are on. On macOS, open System Settings, Privacy & Security, Microphone, and switch Zoom on — then quit and reopen Zoom, because the permission only takes effect on restart.

  7. Check whether the host muted you

    Hosts can mute participants and can restrict unmuting entirely. If your microphone icon is greyed out rather than simply red, you cannot unmute yourself; use “Raise Hand” and ask the host. This is a meeting setting, not a fault.

  8. Leave and rejoin with computer audio

    Leave the meeting and rejoin, choosing “Join with Computer Audio” at the prompt. If the audio subsystem got into a bad state, this clears it faster than any settings change. Ticking “Automatically join audio by computer when joining a meeting” in Settings, Audio prevents it happening again.

Join Audio versus mute — telling them apart

These two states look similar and have completely different fixes. If the bottom-left button reads “Join Audio” with a headphone icon, you are connected to the meeting but not to its audio channel at all: you cannot hear anyone and nobody can hear you.

If the button shows a microphone with a red slash, audio is connected and you are simply muted. Click to unmute, or hold space bar to talk temporarily. A greyed-out icon means the host has restricted unmuting and only they can release it.

Original sound for music and instruments

Zoom is tuned for speech. Its noise suppression, echo cancellation and automatic gain actively work against music, causing instruments to fade in and out and sustained notes to be treated as background noise.

If you teach music, sing, or play an instrument on Zoom, enable “Original sound for musicians” in Settings, Audio, Advanced. It disables the speech processing chain and gives you a raw signal. Use headphones when you do, because echo cancellation is disabled along with everything else.

When the microphone works but you sound bad

A working microphone that sounds poor on Zoom usually comes down to three things. Bluetooth earbuds drop to a low-quality mono profile the moment their microphone is used, which is why AirPods sound noticeably worse on calls than for music. Automatic volume adjustment pumps the level up and down mid-sentence. And a laptop's built-in array microphone picks up the room, the fan and the keyboard along with your voice.

A cheap wired headset with the level set manually beats an expensive wireless one on a call almost every time.

Zoom Microphone Not Working — FAQ

Why can't anyone hear me on Zoom?

Check the bottom-left button first. If it says “Join Audio” you never connected to the meeting's audio; if it shows a red slash you are muted. Both are one click to fix.

How do I test my microphone in Zoom?

Settings, Audio, then “Test Mic”. Speak and listen to the playback. The test at the top of this page does the same thing before you even open Zoom.

Zoom picked the wrong microphone.

Click the arrow beside the microphone icon in the meeting and pick the right input. Zoom keeps its own device selection, separate from your operating system.

Why do I keep cutting out on Zoom?

Usually background noise suppression set to Auto or High removing quiet speech, or automatic volume adjustment. Set suppression to Low and the level manually.

Do I need to restart Zoom after granting permission on Mac?

Yes. macOS only applies a newly granted microphone permission when the application is restarted.

How do I get better sound quality on Zoom?

Use a wired headset, turn off automatic volume adjustment, set noise suppression to Low, and enable original sound if you are playing music.
