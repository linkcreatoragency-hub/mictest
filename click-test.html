Click Speed Test — How Many Clicks Per Second?

How the test works

The timer does not start when the page loads — it starts on your very first click, so there is no wasted reaction time and no advantage in anticipating a countdown. From that moment the clock runs for the duration you chose and every mouse-down event inside the panel is counted. At the end, the score is simply clicks divided by seconds.

Counting mouse-down rather than full clicks matters. A full click event only fires when the button goes down and comes back up in the same place, which means very fast clicking can lose events. Counting the press itself gives a truer picture of how quickly your finger is actually working.

Why your mouse limits your score

Three hardware factors cap what you can achieve regardless of your hand.

Debounce time. Every mouse deliberately ignores signals that arrive within a few milliseconds of the previous one, because mechanical switches bounce as they close. Manufacturers pick a value — often between 8 and 20 ms — and anything faster than that is filtered out as noise. A long debounce is safer against false clicks and harder on a click-speed score.

Actuation force and travel. A switch that needs 70 grams of force and 1.5 mm of travel simply cannot cycle as fast as one needing 45 grams and 0.8 mm. Mice marketed for clicking speed use light, short-travel switches, and increasingly optical switches that have no mechanical contact to bounce at all.

Polling rate. At 125 Hz the mouse reports to the computer every 8 ms. At 1000 Hz it reports every millisecond. For counting clicks the difference is small, but at very high click rates a low polling rate can merge two presses into one report.

A word about technique and your hands

The techniques that push scores past 10 CPS work by turning a voluntary movement into an involuntary one. Jitter clicking tenses the forearm until the hand shakes, and the shake does the clicking. That is, quite literally, deliberately inducing a tremor, and people who do it heavily report wrist and forearm pain.

Butterfly clicking alternates two fingers on the same button, which is easier on the tendons but produces irregular timing that many games detect and penalise. Drag clicking, which relies on friction across the button surface, wears out switches quickly.

If you feel pain, tingling or numbness while doing any of these, stop. A click-speed score is not worth a repetitive strain injury, and the damage accumulates quietly before it announces itself.

If your score seems wrong

A score noticeably lower than your clicking felt usually means clicks are being lost. The first thing to check is whether your mouse has developed the opposite problem — a switch that fires twice from one press. Both are symptoms of the same wear. The double click test measures the gap between consecutive clicks in milliseconds and tells you within about ten clicks whether your switch is chattering.

Click speed test FAQ

What is a good clicks-per-second score?

Most people land between 4 and 7 CPS clicking normally. Above 8 is fast, and above 10 usually means a specialised technique such as jitter clicking or butterfly clicking rather than ordinary pressing.

What is the difference between normal, jitter and butterfly clicking?

Normal clicking uses one finger pressing the switch. Jitter clicking tenses the arm to produce a rapid tremor, which raises the rate but is hard on the tendons. Butterfly clicking alternates two fingers on the same button. Many games ban the faster techniques.

Does my mouse affect my score?

Yes. Switch travel, actuation force and debounce time all change how quickly repeated presses register. A mouse with a long debounce filter deliberately ignores very fast repeats, which caps your measured rate no matter how fast your finger moves.

Can I use this on a touchscreen?

Yes. Taps count the same as clicks, so the test works on phones and tablets, though scores are usually lower than with a mouse.

Is fast clicking bad for my hand?

It can be. Jitter clicking in particular deliberately induces tremor through tension and has been linked to repetitive strain problems. If anything hurts or tingles, stop.

Why did my count seem lower than my clicking felt?

Either your mouse debounce is filtering the fastest presses, or the switch is missing clicks as it wears. The double click test is the quickest way to tell which.
